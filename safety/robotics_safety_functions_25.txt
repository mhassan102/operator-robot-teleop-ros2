The 25 Mandatory and Conditional Safety Functions
=================================================
Source: diagram of robotics safety functions classified by zone.
Tags: [MAND] = Mandatory, [COND] = Conditional.
Note: The source diagram title says "25" functions. The visible boxes total 23.
      "Speed & Separation Control" has no [MAND]/[COND] tag in the source diagram.


ZONE 1 — Stopping & Restart
---------------------------

1. Normal Stop                          [MAND]
   Definition:
     Controlled stop that finishes the current motion cleanly, then holds.
   Example:
     Operator hits “Stop cycle” on a pick-and-place arm; the gripper finishes
     the place, then the arm parks.

2. Protective Stop                      [MAND]
   Definition:
     Faster stop when a safety sensor trips, without cutting all power if
     possible.
   Example:
     Light curtain breaks while a cobot is moving; motion stops immediately
     but servo power stays so it can resume after clear.

3. E-Stop                               [COND]
   Definition:
     Hard, immediate stop (often Category 0/1) from a dedicated button or
     pendant.
   Example:
     Teleoperator slams the red button when the remote arm swings toward a
     person.

4. Start/Restart Interlock              [MAND]
   Definition:
     Robot cannot start or resume until a deliberate, safe start action is
     given.
   Example:
     After a protective stop, the arm will not move until the operator presses
     “Restart” on the teach pendant.

5. Reset                                [COND]
   Definition:
     Clears a latched safety fault only after the hazard is gone and a reset
     is confirmed.
   Example:
     After an E-stop, reset is allowed only when all doors are closed and the
     E-stop is pulled out.

6. Monitored Stationary Position        [COND]
   Definition:
     Confirms the robot is truly stopped and stays in that pose.
   Example:
     During a tool change, the controller verifies joint velocities are ~0
     and holds position while a human is nearby.


ZONE 2 — Stability & Locomotion
-------------------------------

7.  Loss-of-Stability Risk Reduction    [COND]
    Definition:
      Limits speed, payload, or lean before the platform can tip.
    Example:
      A mobile manipulator slows and lowers its arm when the CoM approaches
      the support polygon.

8.  Dynamic Stability Control           [COND]
    Definition:
      Active balancing while moving (braking, posture, wheel torque).
    Example:
      A two-wheel or quadruped robot counters a shove by shifting weight and
      adjusting gait.

9.  Onset-of-Instability Detection      [COND]
    Definition:
      Detects early signs of tipping or slip.
    Example:
      IMU + wheel slip show the base starting to pitch; the system flags
      “instability onset.”

10. Unrecoverable Loss-of-Stability Detection  [COND]
    Definition:
      Declares that recovery is no longer possible and forces a safe reaction
      (stop / drop payload / alarm).
    Example:
      A forklift AMR is already past the tip-over point; it dumps the lift
      command and sounds an alarm rather than trying to “drive out of it.”

11. Elevation-Change Detection          [COND]
    Definition:
      Detects ramps, curbs, stairs, drop-offs.
    Example:
      Depth camera sees a 20 cm drop; navigation refuses that path.

12. Navigation Risk Reduction           [COND]
    Definition:
      Slows, reroutes, or stops when the planned path is hazardous.
    Example:
      Corridor is crowded; the AMR reduces speed and keeps extra clearance.


ZONE 3 — Perception & Collaboration
-----------------------------------

13. Speed & Separation Control          (tag not shown in source)
    Definition:
      Speed drops as a person gets closer (ISO/TS 15066 style).
    Example:
      Cobot runs at 250 mm/s when a human is 1.5 m away, and near-zero when
      they enter the work cell.

14. Contact-Force Limiting              [COND]
    Definition:
      Caps force/torque if contact happens.
    Example:
      Teleoperated gripper or elbow hits a table; torque sensors cut force
      below a bruise threshold.

15. Perception Safeguarding             [COND]
    Definition:
      Uses cameras/lidar/safety scanners so the robot only moves if the scene
      is verified safe.
    Example:
      3D camera must see a clear zone before the arm is allowed to enter
      collaborative mode.


ZONE 4 — Power & Interfaces
---------------------------

16. Hazardous Energy Isolation          [MAND]
    Definition:
      Disconnects dangerous energy (electrical, pneumatic, hydraulic) for
      service.
    Example:
      LOTO switch kills 48 V and vents air so a technician can change a
      gripper.

17. Stored-Energy Control               [MAND]
    Definition:
      Bleeds or locks residual energy (springs, capacitors, accumulators,
      raised axes).
    Example:
      After power-off, a vertical axis is braked and a gas-spring is locked
      so it cannot drop.

18. Loss of Power Protection            [MAND]
    Definition:
      Safe reaction if mains or battery dies mid-motion.
    Example:
      Power cut; brakes engage and the arm does not free-fall onto a person.

19. Minimum-Charge Interlock            [MAND]
    Definition:
      Blocks motion or high-risk modes if battery/charge is too low to finish
      a safe stop.
    Example:
      AMR will not leave the dock if SoC is below the reserve needed to return
      or stop safely.


ZONE 5 — System, Fleet & AI
---------------------------

20. Mode Selection                      [COND]
    Definition:
      Explicit, safe switch between Auto / Manual / Teach / Teleop /
      Maintenance.
    Example:
      Teleop is only enabled when the key-switch is in “Remote” and local
      E-stop is armed.

21. Fleet Safety Command                [MAND]
    Definition:
      Central or supervisor command that can stop or constrain many robots
      at once.
    Example:
      Warehouse fleet manager issues “all units protective stop” when an
      aisle is blocked.

22. AI-Based Safety Control             [COND]
    Definition:
      AI planner/policy is constrained or overridden by a safety layer.
    Example:
      LLM/vision planner proposes a fast swing; the safety supervisor rejects
      it because a person is in the swept volume.

23. Attachment/Payload Stability Check  [COND]
    Definition:
      Verifies tool/payload mass, CoG, and latch before motion.
    Example:
      Arm refuses to lift until the gripper reports “latched” and payload
      estimate is within the stability table.
